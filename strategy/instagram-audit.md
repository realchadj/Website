## 1. The diagnosis, in one paragraph

You are not suffering from a reach problem. You are suffering from an **identity
problem that has caused a reach problem**. A stranger who lands on your page today
sees a handle that says Chad Johnson, a name field that says John, and a bio that
says your name might be Brian, or Brain, or John, and they can call you whatever
they like. There is no statement of who you help, no reason to follow, no offer,
and no next step. Meanwhile the content is unstructured personal philosophy tagged
`#lyfe` — a hashtag with no search demand — interspersed with grievance posts
about producers and agents. Instagram has correctly concluded it does not know who
to show your posts to, and your 193K legacy followers (acquired 2016–2020 for
reality-TV drama) have correctly concluded this is no longer the account they
followed. The 94% engagement collapse is those two facts multiplying.

The good news is that this is the *most fixable* kind of broken. You have 193K
followers, 650,000 press articles of name recognition, a genuinely compelling
second-act story, and a real business. None of that is damaged. It is just not
being pointed at anything.

#### P0-3. You are building a peptide business on rented land that specifically evicts peptide businesses

**Impact: existential. This is the one that can cost you the whole 193K overnight.**

I'd be doing you a disservice to write you a growth plan without saying this
plainly first. Meta classifies most peptides as **unapproved pharmaceuticals**,
and Facebook/Instagram enforce that category more aggressively than almost any
other. As of 2026 the regulatory picture is genuinely in motion — an FDA advisory
panel voted in July 2026 to recommend adding six peptides (BPC-157, TB-500, KPV,
MOTS-c, Epitalon, Semax) to the 503A compounding list — but **that recommendation
is not a rule yet**, and could take a year or more to become one. Meta's
classifiers have not moved at all. Neither has the FTC, which evaluates the *net
impression* of your marketing, meaning a "research use only" disclaimer under
content that plainly implies human use is not a defence — it's an exhibit.

None of this means don't build. It means **build so that losing the account is
survivable**, and so that the account is not the thing that triggers the loss.

**Exact fixes — treat these as house rules, not suggestions:**

1. **Never put a molecule name and a benefit claim in the same post.** Not in the
   caption, not in on-screen text, not in the audio. This is the single pattern
   their automated review is trained on.
2. **No needles on camera.** No vials, no injection footage, no reconstitution
   tutorials in feed. Ever.
3. **No before/after imagery framed medically.** Meta's 2026 health policy
   restricts this outright for supplements and weight loss. Your rebuild story can
   absolutely use before/after — frame it as *life*, not *treatment*. "This is me
   at my worst" is fine. "This is me at my worst, before [molecule]" is a strike.
4. **The transaction never happens on Instagram.** No prices in captions, no
   "DM me to order," no product links in feed. Instagram's job is attention and
   story. The sale happens on your own domain.
5. **Do not run Meta paid ads for the peptide line from any Business Manager you
   care about.** One violation can take the ad account, the pixel, the domain and
   the page with it. If you ever do paid, isolate it completely.
6. **Build the email and SMS list from day one.** This is the highest-ROI item in
   this entire document. 193K followers you don't own is a number. 8,000 email
   addresses you do own is a business. Every story, every week, should have a
   capture path.
7. **Lead with the telehealth lane in public.** A licensed prescriber and a 503A
   compounding relationship is the version of this business that is defensible,
   marketable, and survives a rule change. Retail "research use only" is the lane
   that draws FDA and FTC attention. You can run both; only market one.

I'm not going to keep re-litigating this — you know your business and your
counsel better than I do. But every content and funnel recommendation below is
built assuming these seven rules, because a plan that ignores them is a plan to
lose 193K followers in a single afternoon.

capture ("The rebuild protocol — free, one email"). The email capture goes
*above* the doors because it converts the 90% who aren't ready today, and because
of P0-3 rule 6.

#### P2-4. Visual identity — no system

**Impact: medium — perceived quality, and quality is the whole game in health.**

You are asking people to buy something they put in their body. In that category,

  not sunglasses. At 110px, anything else is a grey smudge.
