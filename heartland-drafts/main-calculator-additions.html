Reconstitution calculator, mixing calculator, dosage calculator: one formula

People search for this tool under many names: peptide reconstitution calculator, peptide mixing calculator, bac water calculator, peptide dosage calculator or just peptide calc. They all come down to two lines:

  1. Concentration (mg/mL) = peptide mass in the vial (mg) ÷ bacteriostatic water added (mL)
  2. Aliquot volume (mL) = amount wanted (mg) ÷ concentration. On a U-100 syringe, units = mL × 100.

Example: a 10 mg vial with 2 mL is 5 mg/mL. An aliquot of 300 mcg (0.3 mg) is 0.06 mL, or 6 units. The calculator above does both steps and warns when an aliquot is too small to measure accurately.

Bac water calculator: how much bacteriostatic water to add

No volume is universally "right". Choose the volume that turns your aliquots into sizes you can measure accurately, usually a few units or more on a U-100 syringe. If an aliquot comes out under 2 units, add more water. For the diluent itself, see bacteriostatic water for peptides. For the full procedure, see how to reconstitute peptides.

What is a peptide calculator?

A peptide calculator works out the concentration of a reconstituted peptide (mass in the vial divided by the diluent volume added) and turns that into syringe measurements: micrograms per unit on a U-100 syringe and the volume of any chosen aliquot. It is also called a peptide reconstitution calculator, a peptide mixing calculator or a bac water calculator. The math is the same under every name.

Is a peptide dosage calculator the same as a reconstitution calculator?

The arithmetic is the same: concentration = mass ÷ volume, then aliquot volume = amount ÷ concentration. "Dosage calculator" is simply what many people search for. This calculator reports concentrations and volumes for laboratory preparation records. It does not recommend any dose, and every product Heartland sells is for research use only.

How do I use a peptide calculator with an insulin syringe?

A U-100 insulin-type syringe is marked in units, and 100 units equals 1 mL, so 1 unit is 0.01 mL. Enter the vial mass and diluent volume to get the concentration. The calculator then shows how many micrograms each unit contains and how many units a chosen aliquot fills. Units are a volume, not an amount of peptide.

How much bacteriostatic water do I need to reconstitute a peptide?

There is no fixed amount. The volume sets the concentration. 1–3 mL is typical for standard 3 mL vials; more water makes each aliquot larger and easier to measure, less water concentrates it. The reconstitution chart on this page shows the concentration for common vial sizes at 1, 2, 3 and 5 mL.
