Yes. GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) is a peptide hormone, an incretin. Its active forms are GLP-1(7-37), 31 amino acids long, and GLP-1(7-36) amide, 30 amino acids long. Intestinal L-cells cut it from the precursor proglucagon, and it acts on the GLP-1 receptor, a class B G protein-coupled receptor.

That short chain is the reason semaglutide, tirzepatide and retatrutide are peptides too. Each one starts from a natural gut-hormone sequence and adds a few changes so it lasts days in circulation instead of minutes.

What Makes GLP-1 a Peptide

A peptide is a chain of amino acids joined by peptide bonds. GLP-1 is exactly that and nothing else: no sugar groups, no lipid, no metal. Here is the human sequence of GLP-1(7-37), taken from the UniProt entry for proglucagon (P01275):

HAEGTFTSDVSSYLEGQAAKEFIAWLVKGRG

That is 31 residues, starting with histidine. GLP-1(7-36) amide stops one residue earlier, at arginine, and carries a C-terminal amide (...WLVKGR-NH2). The odd numbering comes from history. The first form identified was GLP-1(1-37). The active hormone lacks the first six residues, so its numbering starts at 7.

Where GLP-1 Comes From

GLP-1 is not made on its own. The GCG gene encodes a 180-amino-acid precursor, preproglucagon, and different tissues cut it in different places:

  • Intestinal L-cells, found mostly in the distal ileum and colon, use the enzyme prohormone convertase 1/3 (PC1/3). It releases GLP-1, GLP-2, glicentin and oxyntomodulin.
  • Pancreatic alpha cells use prohormone convertase 2 (PC2). It releases glucagon instead.
  • A small group of brainstem neurons (nucleus tractus solitarii) also make GLP-1.

In humans, nearly all circulating GLP-1 is one of the two truncated forms: about 80% is GLP-1(7-36) amide and about 20% is GLP-1(7-37). The two are equally potent at stimulating insulin secretion in published studies.

Native GLP-1 does not last long. Its half-life is around 1 to 2 minutes. The enzyme dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) removes the first two residues (His-Ala), leaving GLP-1(9-36) amide or GLP-1(9-37), and the kidneys clear what is left. That short life is the problem every GLP-1 drug had to solve.

What Type of Hormone Is GLP-1?

GLP-1 is a peptide hormone in the glucagon family, and it is classed as an incretin: a gut hormone released after a meal that increases glucose-dependent insulin secretion. Its receptor, GLP-1R, is a class B G protein-coupled receptor. It has seven transmembrane helices, a large extracellular domain, and signals mainly through Gs and cyclic AMP. Glucagon, GLP-2, GIP and secretin all use receptors from the same class B family.

Is Semaglutide a Peptide?

Yes. Semaglutide is a modified GLP-1(7-37) peptide. Its FDA label describes it as a GLP-1 analogue with 94% sequence homology to human GLP-1, whose peptide backbone is produced by yeast fermentation. The label lists three changes, using GLP-1 numbering:

  • Position 8: alanine is replaced to resist DPP-4. The replacement is aminoisobutyric acid (Aib), a non-standard amino acid.
  • Position 26: the lysine carries a hydrophilic spacer and a C18 fatty diacid. The fatty acid binds serum albumin, which is the main reason semaglutide lasts so long.
  • Position 34: a minor change (lysine to arginine) so that only one fatty diacid can attach.

The label gives the molecular formula as C187H291N45O59, the molecular weight as 4113.58 g/mol, and the elimination half-life as about 1 week. The added side chain makes semaglutide a lipidated peptide, but it is still a peptide.

Is Tirzepatide a Peptide?

Yes. FDA's clinical pharmacology review describes tirzepatide as a 39-amino-acid synthetic peptide engineered from the GIP sequence that includes a C20 fatty diacid moiety. It activates both the GIP and GLP-1 receptors. The review reports an elimination half-life of about 5 days. So tirzepatide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist, but its sequence is based on GIP, not GLP-1.

Is Retatrutide a Peptide?

Yes. The phase 1b study published in The Lancet (2022) describes retatrutide (LY3437943) as a 39-amino-acid single peptide conjugated to a C20 fatty diacid, with agonist activity at the glucagon, GIP and GLP-1 receptors. The same study reported a half-life of about 6 days. Retatrutide is investigational and has not been approved by FDA.

Is GLP-1 a Protein or a Peptide?

It is a peptide. Biochemists have no single cutoff between peptides and proteins, but a 30- or 31-residue hormone falls clearly on the peptide side. US regulation draws a firm line: FDA defines a protein as an alpha amino acid polymer with a specific, defined sequence that is greater than 40 amino acids (21 CFR 600.3(h)(6)). Native GLP-1 (30 to 31), semaglutide (31), tirzepatide (39) and retatrutide (39) are all under that line.

The precursor is different. Preproglucagon, at 180 amino acids, is a protein. GLP-1 is one of the peptides cut from it.

FAQ

Is GLP-1 a peptide hormone?

Yes. GLP-1 is a 30- or 31-amino-acid peptide hormone of the glucagon family. It is released by intestinal L-cells and acts on the GLP-1 receptor, a class B G protein-coupled receptor.

Is semaglutide a peptide?

Yes. Semaglutide is a modified GLP-1 peptide with 94% sequence homology to human GLP-1. It has an Aib substitution at position 8 and a C18 fatty diacid on lysine 26 that binds albumin.

Is tirzepatide a peptide?

Yes. Tirzepatide is a 39-amino-acid synthetic peptide engineered from the GIP sequence, with a C20 fatty diacid. It activates both the GIP and GLP-1 receptors.

What is the amino acid sequence of GLP-1?

Human GLP-1(7-37) is HAEGTFTSDVSSYLEGQAAKEFIAWLVKGRG (31 residues). GLP-1(7-36) amide ends at the arginine and carries a C-terminal amide (30 residues).

Is GLP-1 a protein?

No. GLP-1 is a peptide. It is cut from a 180-amino-acid precursor protein, preproglucagon, but at 30 to 31 residues it is well under the more-than-40-amino-acid threshold FDA uses to define a protein.

Are GLP-1s and peptides the same thing?

No. GLP-1 and the drugs modeled on it are one small group within the much larger peptide category. When clinics contrast "GLP-1s" with "peptides," the second term usually means other compounds.
