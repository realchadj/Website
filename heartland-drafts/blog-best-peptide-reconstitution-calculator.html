The best peptide reconstitution calculator is one you can check. We ran nine tools on one case: a 10 mg vial, 2 mL of diluent and a 500 mcg aliquot. The correct result is 5 mg/mL, 0.1 mL, 10 units on a U-100 syringe and 20 aliquots. Two tools let us confirm it end to end.

This post judges calculators on arithmetic and documentation only. It is not about what amount to measure, and no calculator, including ours, should be asked that.

How we judged each calculator

A reconstitution calculator does three divisions. What separates tools is everything around the math. We used nine criteria.

  1. Inputs. Vial mass, diluent volume and aliquot mass, with free entry as well as preset buttons.
  2. Syringe scales. Whether it handles 0.3, 0.5 and 1 mL barrels, and whether it knows the difference between a U-100 scale and a U-40 scale.
  3. Outputs. Concentration (mg/mL), volume per aliquot (mL), syringe units and aliquots per vial.
  4. Blend support. Whether it can split one vial that holds two compounds.
  5. Formula shown. Whether the page states the equations, so you can check the result without trusting the code.
  6. Presets and shareable inputs. Whether the inputs can be saved in a URL or record.
  7. Mobile use. Tap-sized preset buttons. All eight web tools have them. We checked page structure, not physical devices.
  8. Ads and sign-up walls. Anything you must dismiss or fill in.
  9. Dosing push. Whether the page frames its inputs as personal doses, suggests amounts or sells protocols.

We loaded each tool in a page-extraction browser. Where a tool accepted inputs in the URL, we loaded the test case and read the result. Where it needed a click, we recorded "not run" and checked only what the page shows. We did not guess results.

How to check any calculator's math yourself

You don't need to trust any tool. Four lines of arithmetic will verify it:

  1. Concentration (mg/mL) = vial mass (mg) ÷ diluent volume (mL)
  2. Volume per aliquot (mL) = aliquot mass (mcg) ÷ (concentration × 1,000)
  3. Syringe units = volume (mL) × units per mL on your syringe's scale (100 on U-100, 40 on U-40)
  4. Aliquots per vial = (vial mass × 1,000) ÷ aliquot mass (mcg), rounded down

Worked test case: 10 mg vial, 2 mL diluent, 500 mcg aliquot.

  • Concentration: 10 ÷ 2 = 5 mg/mL (5,000 mcg/mL)
  • Volume: 500 ÷ 5,000 = 0.1 mL
  • U-100 units: 0.1 × 100 = 10 units (on a U-40 scale the same 0.1 mL reads 4 units)
  • Aliquots: 10,000 ÷ 500 = 20

Run that case through any calculator before you rely on it. If it doesn't return all four numbers, stop using it. Record the four numbers on the vial label or in the lab record. The reconstitution guide covers the bench steps.

Common calculator mistakes

  • mg and mcg mixed up. Entering 500 in an mg field when you meant mcg is a thousandfold error. Check which unit each field expects.
  • Syringe capacity mistaken for scale. A 0.5 mL syringe marked "50 units" is still a U-100 scale. Only a true U-40 (or U-20) syringe changes the unit count for the same volume.
  • Total vial capacity used instead of diluent added. Concentration is based on the diluent you actually added, not the vial's size.
  • Label mass treated as exact. The printed vial mass is nominal. Net peptide content can differ from purity, and both come from the lot's certificate of analysis. Heartland publishes COAs in the COA portal. See how to read a COA.
  • Rounding hidden. A tool that shows "3 units" when the true value is 3.3 hides a 10% difference. Prefer tools that show a decimal.
  • Volumes below about 2 units. At 0.02 mL or less, graduation error becomes a large share of the measurement. More diluent gives the same aliquot mass in a larger, more readable volume.
  • Blend vials split wrong. In a two-compound vial, each compound's concentration is its own mass divided by the total diluent. Calculate each one separately.

FAQ

What is the best peptide reconstitution calculator?

The best one shows its formula, reports concentration, volume, syringe units and aliquots per vial, and lets you save the exact inputs. In our test, Heartland Bio Labs and Rite Aid both returned the correct result for a 10 mg vial, 2 mL of diluent and a 500 mcg aliquot, and both accept inputs from a link.

How do I check whether a peptide calculator is accurate?

Run a case you can solve by hand. A 10 mg vial with 2 mL of diluent is 5 mg/mL, so a 500 mcg aliquot is 0.1 mL, 10 units on a U-100 syringe, and the vial holds 20 such aliquots. Any calculator that returns different numbers has an error.

Do 0.3 mL, 0.5 mL and 1 mL syringes give different unit readings?

No, as long as they are all U-100 syringes. One unit is 0.01 mL on each of them. Only the total capacity and the spacing of the marks differ.

What is a U-40 syringe and why does it matter for calculators?

A U-40 syringe is graduated at 40 units per mL instead of 100, so one unit is 0.025 mL. The same 0.1 mL reads 4 units on a U-40 scale and 10 units on a U-100 scale. A calculator that assumes U-100 gives the wrong reading for a U-40 syringe.

Can a peptide calculator handle blend vials?

Some can. Of the web tools we checked, Research Pep Hub has a blend tab, and the PepCalc Android app lists a blend calculator. By hand, divide each compound's mass by the total diluent volume to get each compound's concentration.

Why doesn't the Heartland calculator support U-40 syringes or blends?

It was built for single-compound vials on the U-100 scale, the scale on most insulin-type graduated syringes. For U-40 scales or blend vials, use a tool that supports them, and check the result with the formula on this page.
