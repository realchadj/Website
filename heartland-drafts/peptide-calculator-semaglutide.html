How much bacteriostatic water for semaglutide (2, 5 and 10 mg vials)?

No single volume is correct. What you add sets the concentration, and the concentration sets how large each aliquot is. The math is concentration = semaglutide mass ÷ diluent volume. Each unit on a U-100 syringe is 0.01 mL, so mcg per unit = concentration in mg/mL × 10.

Two limits apply in practice:

  • Small volumes measure poorly. Below about 2 units, the syringe's graduation error is a large share of the reading. More diluent turns the same mass into a larger volume that reads more accurately.
  • Vial headspace is limited. Most research vials are 3 mL vials, which puts the practical ceiling around 3 mL of diluent. To dilute further, transfer the solution to a larger sterile vial.

How to reconstitute semaglutide

Semaglutide ships as lyophilized (freeze-dried) powder because it is stable that way. Reconstitution follows the standard bench procedure:

  1. Let it warm up. Bring the sealed vial to room temperature. Wipe the stoppers of the semaglutide vial and the bacteriostatic water vial with alcohol.
  2. Draw the diluent. With a sterile syringe, draw the volume you chose above.
  3. Add it slowly, down the glass. Aim the stream at the inside wall of the vial, not at the powder. A direct jet can shear the peptide.
  4. Swirl it; don't shake it. Roll the vial gently until the solution is clear. If it stays cloudy or has particles, it hasn't fully dissolved or the material has degraded.
  5. Label and refrigerate. Write down the date, lot number and the concentration the calculator gave. Store at 2–8 °C.

Semaglutide carries a C18 fatty diacid side chain but dissolves in bacteriostatic water with gentle swirling. Give it a minute or two before deciding it hasn't dissolved. For materials, technique and storage in more depth, see the full guide: how to reconstitute peptides.

Storage after reconstitution

Keep lyophilized semaglutide sealed, cold, dry and away from light, and refrigerate or freeze it for longer storage. Once reconstituted with bacteriostatic water, store it at 2–8 °C. Bacteriostatic preparations are conventionally used within about 28 days. Avoid repeated temperature swings and agitation.

How do you convert a semaglutide amount in mg to units on a syringe?

Divide the amount by the concentration to get millilitres, then multiply by 100 to get U-100 units. At 2.5 mg/mL, 0.3 mg is 0.12 mL, which is 12 units. Units measure volume. The mass in each unit depends on the concentration you prepared.

How long does reconstituted semaglutide last?

Reconstituted with bacteriostatic water and kept at 2–8 °C, it is conventionally used within about 28 days. Keep lyophilized powder sealed, cold, dry and away from light. Avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles.

Why is my reconstituted semaglutide cloudy?

Cloudiness or particles after gentle swirling mean the powder hasn't fully dissolved or the material has degraded. Common causes are shaking the vial, spraying diluent straight onto the powder, or adding diluent to a cold vial. Solution that stays cloudy is not used.

Does adding more water change how much semaglutide is in the vial?

No. A 2 mg vial holds 2 mg whether you add 1 mL or 3 mL. Water changes only how much of that mass is in each millilitre.
