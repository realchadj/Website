How much bacteriostatic water for 10 mg retatrutide (and 20 or 30 mg)?

No single volume is correct. What you add sets the concentration, and the concentration sets how large each aliquot is. The math is concentration = retatrutide mass ÷ diluent volume. Each unit on a U-100 syringe is 0.01 mL, so mcg per unit = concentration in mg/mL × 10.

Two limits apply in practice:

  • Small volumes measure poorly. Below about 2 units, the syringe's graduation error is a large share of the reading. More diluent turns the same mass into a larger volume that reads more accurately.
  • Vial headspace is limited. Most research vials are 3 mL vials, which puts the practical ceiling around 3 mL of diluent. To dilute further, transfer the solution to a larger sterile vial.

How to reconstitute retatrutide

Retatrutide ships as lyophilized (freeze-dried) powder because it is stable that way. Reconstitution follows the standard bench procedure:

  1. Let it warm up. Bring the sealed vial to room temperature. Wipe the stoppers of the retatrutide vial and the bacteriostatic water vial with alcohol.
  2. Draw the diluent. With a sterile syringe, draw the volume you chose above.
  3. Add it slowly, down the glass. Aim the stream at the inside wall of the vial, not at the powder. A direct jet can shear the peptide.
  4. Swirl it; don't shake it. Roll the vial gently until the solution is clear. If it stays cloudy or has particles, it hasn't fully dissolved or the material has degraded.
  5. Label and refrigerate. Write down the date, lot number and the concentration the calculator gave. Store at 2–8 °C.

For materials, technique and storage in more depth, see the full guide: how to reconstitute peptides.

Storage after reconstitution

Lyophilized retatrutide keeps long-term at −20 °C, sealed and protected from light and moisture. Once reconstituted with bacteriostatic water, store it at 2–8 °C and use it within 28 days. That window is what the 0.9% benzyl alcohol in bacteriostatic water supports. Avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles and vigorous agitation, which both degrade peptide solutions.

Retatrutide + tirzepatide blend vial math

A blend vial holds two peptides. Diluent dilutes both, so each one has its own concentration. Run the calculation once for each component, using that component's mass. For the retatrutide 20 mg / tirzepatide 40 mg blend reconstituted with 3 mL:

  • Retatrutide: 20 mg ÷ 3 mL = 6.67 mg/mL (66.7 mcg per unit)
  • Tirzepatide: 40 mg ÷ 3 mL = 13.3 mg/mL (133 mcg per unit)

The ratio between the two is fixed by the fill (1 : 2 here) and stays the same whatever volume you use. Enter each component's mass in the calculator above to get its figures.

How do you convert a retatrutide amount in mg to units on a syringe?

Divide the amount by the concentration to get millilitres, then multiply by 100 to get U-100 units. At 5 mg/mL, 0.3 mg is 0.06 mL, which is 6 units. Units measure volume. The mass in each unit depends on the concentration you prepared.

How long does reconstituted retatrutide last?

Reconstituted with bacteriostatic water and kept at 2–8 °C, it is conventionally used within 28 days. Lyophilized powder is stored at −20 °C, protected from light and moisture. Avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles.

Why is my reconstituted retatrutide cloudy?

Cloudiness or particles after gentle swirling mean the powder hasn't fully dissolved or the material has degraded. Common causes are shaking the vial, spraying diluent straight onto the powder, or adding diluent to a cold vial. Solution that stays cloudy is not used.

Does adding more water change how much retatrutide is in the vial?

No. A 10 mg vial holds 10 mg whether you add 1 mL or 3 mL. Water changes only how much of that mass is in each millilitre.
