GHRP-2 and GHRP-6 are the two original growth hormone releasing peptides, and they are usually discussed as a pair because they are so alike: both are six residues long, both act on the same receptor, and both came out of the same line of research. The differences between them are small in structure and meaningful in the lab. This comparison covers what GHRP-2 and GHRP-6 are, where they diverge, and how each is verified.

What the GHRPs Are

Growth hormone releasing peptides are synthetic hexapeptides developed in the late 1970s and early 1980s, most closely associated with the work of Cyril Bowers. They were designed before their target was known. Researchers later found that they act on the growth hormone secretagogue receptor (GHS-R1a), and in 1999 the natural hormone for that receptor was discovered: ghrelin. The GHRPs therefore sit on the ghrelin receptor side of the growth hormone axis, alongside ipamorelin, and opposite the GHRH receptor compounds such as sermorelin and CJC-1295. Where They Differ

GHRP-6 is the earlier molecule. GHRP-2 was developed from the same template by replacing the first two residues, swapping histidine and D-tryptophan for D-alanine and D-2-naphthylalanine, an unnatural aromatic amino acid. In published comparisons that change is associated with greater potency at releasing growth hormone. GHRP-6 is the compound more often chosen when researchers want to model ghrelin's appetite signaling, since that effect is especially pronounced with it. GHRP-2 has also been used as a diagnostic agent in Japan under the name pralmorelin.

Both are less selective than ipamorelin, a later pentapeptide designed to release growth hormone with minimal effect on cortisol and prolactin. Researchers often use the three as a selectivity series: GHRP-6, GHRP-2, then ipamorelin. Our ipamorelin overview covers the selective end of that series.

Stability and Handling

Both hexapeptides are amidated at the C terminus and contain D amino acids, which makes them more resistant to enzyme breakdown than natural peptides of the same length. Both contain tryptophan, which is sensitive to oxidation and light. Store lyophilized material cold, dry, and dark, let vials reach room temperature before opening, and keep solutions refrigerated and aliquoted. Quality Verification

The analytical challenge with the GHRPs is stereochemistry. A batch where a D residue was accidentally built in as its L form has exactly the same mass as the correct peptide, so mass spectrometry alone cannot catch it. A well developed HPLC method can usually separate those diastereomers, which is why purity by HPLC and identity by mass spectrometry are always reported together. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between GHRP-2 and GHRP-6?

They share the same receptor and the same last four residues. GHRP-2 replaces GHRP-6's first two residues with D-alanine and D-2-naphthylalanine, which is associated with higher growth hormone releasing potency, while GHRP-6 shows stronger ghrelin-like appetite signaling in animal models.

What receptor do GHRPs act on?

The growth hormone secretagogue receptor, GHS-R1a, which is also the receptor for the natural hormone ghrelin.

Is GHRP-2 the same as pralmorelin?

Yes. Pralmorelin is the international nonproprietary name for GHRP-2.

How is GHRP-6 different from ipamorelin?

Both act on the ghrelin receptor, but ipamorelin is a pentapeptide designed to be more selective, with much less effect on cortisol, prolactin, and appetite signaling in published studies.
