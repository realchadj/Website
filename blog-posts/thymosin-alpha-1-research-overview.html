Thymosin Alpha 1 is the peptide researchers most often confuse with a compound it has almost nothing in common with. Despite sharing the "thymosin" name with thymosin beta 4, the parent of TB-500, it comes from a different gene, has a different structure, and is studied in a different field. This overview covers what Thymosin Alpha 1 is, where it came from, how it differs from its namesake, and how research grade material is verified.

What Thymosin Alpha 1 Is

Thymosin Alpha 1 (Tα1) is a 28 amino acid peptide with an acetylated N terminus and a molecular weight of about 3,108 Da. In nature it is cut from a larger precursor protein, prothymosin alpha. The synthetic form, known by the international name thymalfasin, reproduces that 28 residue sequence including the N terminal acetyl group.

Where It Came From

Tα1 was first isolated in 1977 from thymosin fraction 5, a partially purified extract of calf thymus tissue, by Allan Goldstein and colleagues. Fraction 5 turned out to be a mixture of many peptides, and the "alpha" and "beta" labels were assigned according to where each one ran during separation, not according to any shared function. That naming history is the source of the confusion that still surrounds the thymosins today.

The Immune Research Context

Most published work on Tα1 sits in immunology. Studies have examined its interactions with toll-like receptors on dendritic cells and its effects on T cell maturation and cytokine signaling in cell and animal models. Thymalfasin has been approved as a medicine in a number of countries outside the United States; it is not approved by the FDA. That regulatory history is part of why the molecule is so well characterized analytically: its identity and purity specifications have been worked out in detail.

Stability and Handling

At 28 residues, Tα1 is a mid sized peptide that is reasonably robust as a dry powder but still sensitive to heat, moisture, and repeated freeze thaw cycling once dissolved. Standard practice applies: lyophilized material stored cold, dry, and dark; solutions refrigerated, handled gently without vigorous shaking, and divided into aliquots. The reconstitution guide and peptide calculator cover preparation.

Quality Verification

For a 28 residue acetylated peptide, the specific risks are incomplete acetylation and deletion sequences missing a single residue, both of which produce near neighbors in the chromatogram. Mass spectrometry confirms the full acetylated mass, HPLC quantifies purity, and net peptide content ties the certificate to the label. Frequently Asked Questions

Is Thymosin Alpha 1 the same as TB-500?

No. TB-500 is derived from thymosin beta 4, a different 43 residue protein. The two share a name because both were first identified in the same thymus extract.

How long is Thymosin Alpha 1?

28 amino acids, with an acetylated N terminus and a molecular weight of about 3,108 Da.

What is thymalfasin?

Thymalfasin is the international nonproprietary name for synthetic Thymosin Alpha 1.

Is Thymosin Alpha 1 FDA approved?

No. It has been approved as a medicine in several other countries, but not in the United States.
