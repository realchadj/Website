A peptide that passes every test on its certificate of analysis can still fail in the lab if it is stored badly. Heat, moisture, light, air, and repeated freezing each break peptides down by different chemical routes, and most of them leave no visible sign in the vial. This guide covers how to store lyophilized and reconstituted research peptides, which residues are most vulnerable, and the handling mistakes that cost the most.

Why Peptides Degrade

Peptide degradation is not one process but several, and each is driven by different conditions:

PathwayResidues most at riskAccelerated by
OxidationMethionine, cysteine, tryptophanAir, light, trace metals
DeamidationAsparagine, glutamine (especially Asn-Gly)Water, heat, neutral to alkaline pH
HydrolysisAspartic acid bonds (Asp-Pro, Asp-Gly)Water, heat, acidic pH
Disulfide scramblingCysteine pairsHeat, higher pH
AggregationHydrophobic sequencesAgitation, freeze thaw, high concentration

Almost every pathway needs water, which is why the dry lyophilized form is so much more stable than any solution.

Storing Lyophilized Peptides

Lyophilization, or freeze drying, removes water so the peptide can be shipped and stored as a stable powder or cake. To keep it that way:

  • Temperature: -20°C is the standard for long term storage. Refrigeration at 2 to 8°C is acceptable for shorter periods, and room temperature is fine for the days a sealed vial spends in transit.
  • Moisture: Keep vials sealed and, ideally, in a container with desiccant. Lyophilized peptides draw moisture from the air readily.
  • Light: Store vials in the dark, especially sequences containing tryptophan.
  • Warm up before opening: Always let a cold vial reach room temperature before opening it. Opening a cold vial pulls humid air onto the powder, and the condensation that forms is the single most common way stored peptides are damaged.

Storing Reconstituted Peptides

Once a peptide is in solution, its stability depends on its sequence and can drop from years to days or weeks. General practice:

  • Refrigerate solutions at 2 to 8°C and protect them from light.
  • Reconstitute with an appropriate solvent. Bacteriostatic water contains 0.9% benzyl alcohol to inhibit microbial growth in multi-use vials; our bacteriostatic water guide covers when it is and isn't the right choice.
  • Add solvent slowly down the side of the vial and swirl gently. Do not shake, since agitation promotes aggregation.
  • If a solution must be kept long term, divide it into single use aliquots and freeze them, rather than freezing and thawing one vial repeatedly.

How Long Do Peptides Last?

There is no single answer, because stability depends on the sequence. As a general guide, a well sealed lyophilized peptide stored at -20°C typically remains stable for years, while solutions are measured in days to weeks under refrigeration. Sequences with the vulnerable residues in the table above sit at the shorter end. For work where exact concentration matters, stability in your specific conditions should be verified rather than assumed.

Peptides That Need Extra Care

  • Cysteine-containing peptides such as glutathione oxidize readily in solution.
  • Disulfide-bridged peptides such as oxytocin are notably heat sensitive.
  • Copper complexes such as GHK-Cu have their own color and pH behavior; see why GHK-Cu turns blue.
  • Unmodified short peptides such as DSIP lack protecting groups at their ends.

Signs a Peptide Has Degraded

Cloudiness, visible particles, a collapsed or sticky cake, or powder that has turned into a film can all indicate moisture exposure or aggregation. Most degradation, however, is invisible: an oxidized or deamidated peptide usually looks identical to an intact one. Only analytical testing can confirm it, which is why storage discipline matters more than visual inspection. Our guide to HPLC and mass spectrometry explains how degradation products are detected.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do lyophilized peptides need to be refrigerated?

For long term storage, yes: -20°C is standard. Sealed lyophilized peptides tolerate room temperature for short periods, such as during shipping.

How should reconstituted peptides be stored?

Refrigerated at 2 to 8°C, protected from light, and aliquoted if they will be kept long enough to need freezing.

Why should I let a peptide vial warm up before opening it?

Opening a cold vial draws humid air inside, and water condenses on the powder. Moisture drives most peptide degradation.

Can peptides be refrozen?

Repeated freeze thaw cycles promote aggregation and degradation. Freeze solutions once, in single use aliquots.
